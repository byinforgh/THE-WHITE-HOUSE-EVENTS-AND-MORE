How This Dashboard Works

Step 1, every shift: tap "Who are you?" at the top and select your name. First time ever, you'll set a 4+ digit PIN — remember it, you'll need it every time you select your name after that. This is what credits bookings, tasks, cleaning checks and notes to you.
Today is your home screen: today's bookings, open tasks, open breakdowns, VIP follow-ups, and who's currently in-house/booked/walk-in, all in one place. Tap any card to jump to the full tab.
Rooms shows every room's live status (Vacant / Booked / Occupied / Cleaning / Maintenance / Out of Service). Tap a room to change its status or add a note.
Bookings: add a new booking here. For a Room booking, assign the room and record deposit/payment right in the form if you can — if not, use the "Room / Payment" button on any booking later. Check In moves a guest from Booked into the room; Check Out sends the room to Cleaning automatically.
Cleaning: log a check per zone. For Bedrooms/Bathrooms you pick the actual room from a list, not free typing. A Fail needs a note on what's wrong and stays flagged red until someone taps "Clear" once it's actually fixed — don't clear it before it's really done.
Tasks: everything active shows together up top; done and cancelled tasks sink into the Archive below so you're not hunting through tabs. "Cancel" is for a task that's no longer needed — don't mark it Done if nothing was actually done.
Notes: a shared notebook for handovers, guest preferences, incidents, reminders. Tap "Archive" once something's been actioned so it stops cluttering the open list — it's still there under the Archived filter if you need to check it later.
The dashboard refreshes itself every minute, and if it's left untouched for 5 minutes it returns to Today on its own — so nobody leaves it stuck on a half-filled form.

The Five-Star Greeting

Every guest greeted within seconds, eye contact, a smile, and their name if it's known ("Good afternoon, welcome back"). The same warmth for every guest regardless of how they arrive or what they're wearing: some of the most important guests won't look like it at first glance.
If you don't know an answer: "Let me get you the right person right now": and walk them there yourself, don't just point.

Membership: Regular vs VIP

Regular: free, automatic, earns points from every order/visit. Offer it, never push it.
VIP: a paid subscription, never granted on the spot. If a guest is interested, log it (Guests & Members → Log VIP Interest) and management follows up to arrange payment. Never tell a guest they're "now VIP" unless a manager has actually confirmed it.

Refund / Comp / Escalation Authority

Tier 1: Any staff: small goodwill gestures (a complimentary drink, a sincere apology, priority attention). No cash value threshold.
Tier 2: Shift lead/supervisor: gate fee refund or re-entry pass, replacing a spoiled order, a service-failure comp up to the owner-set threshold, rebooking a missed slot.
Tier 3: Management only: full refunds, event-related comps, anything involving a VIP/public figure or a safety incident, anything likely to be discussed publicly.
Golden rule: if it's unclear which tier applies, escalate up rather than guess down.

VIP / Traditional & Dignitary Guests

Stay composed and professional: no staring, no phones out. Recognition goes through the correct channel quietly, never a visible reaction on the floor. No staff member initiates a photo/interaction unless the guest offers it first.
For chiefs, queen mothers, or traditional delegations: a senior staff member (not junior front-of-house) does the formal greeting. "Nana" is a safe default form of address while confirming specifics with the delegation's coordinator. No photography of regalia without explicit permission.

Emergency Basics: Know Cold

Pool distress: non-lifeguard staff clear the area, alert the nearest lifeguard and on-call manager immediately. Do not attempt a rescue without training.
Fire: know your nearest extinguisher, evacuation route, and assembly point.
Security incident: alert security/on-call manager first, stay composed: guests take their cue from staff behaviour.

Housekeeping: "Ready" Means

Clean · dust-free · smells fresh · everything in its place · supplies stocked · nothing broken · lights/equipment checked · rubbish removed · nothing left by a previous guest · any problem reported. If one is missing, it's not ready. Full checklists live under the Cleaning tab.

Media / Press On-Site

If a journalist, blogger, or influencer approaches: "I'd love to help: let me connect you with our team for that," then notify management immediately. No staff member confirms who is on property, or gives a quote, without management sign-off.
